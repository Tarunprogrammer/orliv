# Urban Rooftop Organic Farming: Natural Soil Nutrition & Bio-Fertilizers

## 1. Microbial Soil Inoculants & Liquid Formulations

In containerized urban rooftop systems, soil nutrients deplete rapidly due to restricted root volume and frequent irrigation. Chemical fertilizers (synthetic urea, DAP) cause rapid salt buildup, kill soil microbiology, and cause root scorching. Natural bio-ferments replenish live microbes and provide slow-release minerals.

### 1. Liquid Jeevamrut (Jivamrita) - Microbial Biostimulant:
- **Core Principle**: An aerobic culture broth containing billions of indigenous beneficial microbes (*Bacillus*, *Pseudomonas*, fungi, and yeasts) that unlock locked minerals in the potting substrate.
- **Classic 200-Liter Batch Recipe**:
  * **10 kg** Fresh Indigenous (Desi) Cow Dung.
  * **5 to 10 Liters** Cow Urine (Gomutra).
  * **2 kg** Organic Unrefined Jaggery / Molasses (carbon/sugar energy source for microbes).
  * **2 kg** Pulse / Legume Flour (Chickpea / Besan flour for microbial protein).
  * **1 Handful (100g)** Fertile Virgin Soil or Leaf Mold (source of pristine native soil microbial innocula).
  * **200 Liters** Clean, unchlorinated water.
- **Preparation & Fermentation**:
  * Mix all ingredients in a clean plastic drum or earthenware vat kept in total shade.
  * Stir clockwise for 2 to 3 minutes, 2 to 3 times a day for **48 to 72 hours** in summer (or 4 to 6 days in winter).
  * Keep drum loosely covered with a breathable jute gunny bag.
- **Application Method**:
  * Dilute **1:10 with water (10% to 20% concentration)** and apply as a soil drench (200 ml to 500 ml per 12-inch pot) every **10 to 14 days**.
  * Use within 7 days of fermentation completion for maximum viable microbial count.

### 2. Vermiwash Liquid Tonic:
- Rich in coelomic fluid, soluble plant hormones (auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins), vitamins, and trace minerals extracted from active earthworm beds (*Eisenia foetida*).
- **Application**: Dilute **1 Part Vermiwash to 5 Parts Water** as a foliar mist to stimulate flowering, fruit set, and leaf luster.

## 2. Targeted Bio-Fertilizers for Containers

1. **Nitrogen-Fixing Microorganisms**:
   - **Azotobacter chroococcum**: Free-living aerobic nitrogen fixer ideal for non-leguminous vegetables (tomatoes, chillies, leafy greens). Fixes 20–25 kg atmospheric N/hectare equivalent.
   - **Rhizobium**: Symbiotic nitrogen-fixer for leguminous beans and peas.
2. **Phosphate-Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB - *Bacillus megaterium* / *Pseudomonas striata*)**:
   - Secretes organic citric and gluconic acids to convert insoluble bound rock phosphorus into plant-absorbable orthophosphate ions (H₂PO₄⁻).
3. **Potash-Mobilizing Bacteria (KMB - *Frateuria aurantia*)**:
   - Solubilizes complex potassium silicates into bioavailable potassium for fruit bulking and disease resistance.
4. **Vesicular-Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (VAM - *Glomus intraradices*)**:
   - Forms symbiotic hyphal networks extending the container root absorption area by 100x to 1000x, critical for moisture and phosphorus extraction under rooftop heat stress.

## 3. Solid Organic Top-Dressing & Mineral Supplements

Every 30 to 45 days, container crops require solid replenishment around the drip line:
1. **Enriched Vermicompost (200g per 12-inch container)**:
   - Contains balanced NPK (1.5-2.0% N, 1.0-1.5% P, 1.5% K) with high cation-exchange capacity (CEC).
2. **Neem Cake Powder (30–50g per container)**:
   - High in natural organic nitrogen (2–5%), acts as a natural nitrification inhibitor and deters root-knot nematodes (*Meloidogyne incognita*).
3. **Steamed Bone Meal / Soft Rock Phosphate (20g per container)**:
   - Provides slow-release calcium and phosphorus for root anchoring and blossom end rot prevention.
4. **Hardwood Ash (1 tablespoon per container)**:
   - Rich in bioavailable potassium (5–7% K₂O) and calcium carbonate. (Use sparingly to avoid elevating soil pH above 7.0).
5. **Epsom Salt (Magnesium Sulfate - 5g/liter)**:
   - Organic foliar spray to prevent interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between leaf veins) in solanaceous vegetables during heavy fruiting.
