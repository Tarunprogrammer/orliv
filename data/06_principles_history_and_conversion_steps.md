## 1. Organic Farming Definition & Core Meaning

Organic farming is an agricultural system based on Mahatma Gandhi's ethos: *"Nature can provide for everyone's need but not for greed"*.

The term **'organic'** was first coined by **Lord Northbourne in 1940** in his seminal book entitled *'Look to the Land'*.

### Formal Institutional Definitions:
- **IFOAM (International Federation of Organic Agriculture Movements)**: 
  "Organic Agriculture is a production system that sustains the health of soils, ecosystems, and people. It relies on ecological processes, biodiversity and cycles adapted to local conditions, rather than the use of inputs with adverse effects. Organic Agriculture combines tradition, innovation, and science to benefit the shared environment and promote fair relationships and good quality of life for all involved."
- **USDA (United States Department of Agriculture)**:
  "Organic farming is a system which avoids or largely excludes the use of synthetic inputs (such as fertilizers, pesticides, hormones, feed additives etc.) and to the maximum extent feasible relies upon crop rotations, crop residues, animal manures, off-farm organic waste, mineral grade rock additives and biological system of nutrient mobilization and plant protection."
- **FAO (Food and Agriculture Organization)**:
  "Organic agriculture is a unique production management system which promotes and enhances agro-ecosystem health, including biodiversity, biological cycles and soil biological activity, and this is accomplished by using on-farm agronomic, biological and mechanical methods in exclusion of all synthetic off-farm inputs."

## 2. Historical Roots

- **Ancient Roots (1,000+ Years Ago)**: Ancient farmers cultivated river belts utilizing natural inputs only. Indian ancient literature such as the **Rig-Veda, Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Kautilya's Arthashastra** contain detailed documentation of organic inputs and natural manures.
- **Modern Pioneers (Early 1900s)**:
  * **Sir Albert Howard**: Worked extensively as an agricultural researcher in India, drawing profound inspiration from traditional sustainable Indian farming techniques and establishing the scientific Indore composting method.
  * **F.H. King**: Documented Asian natural fertility practices in *'Farmers of Forty Centuries'*.
  * **Rudolf Steiner**: Developed biodynamic agriculture in Europe.
  * **J.I. Rodale**: Popularized and advocated organic gardening and regenerative farming in the West.

---

## 3. Four Core IFOAM Principles of Organic Agriculture

1. **Principle of Health**: Organic agriculture should sustain and enhance the health of soil, plant, animal, human, and planet as one and indivisible.
2. **Principle of Ecology**: Organic agriculture should be based on living ecological systems and cycles, work with them, emulate them, and help sustain them.
3. **Principle of Fairness**: Organic agriculture should build on relationships that ensure fairness with regard to the common environment and life opportunities.
4. **Principle of Care**: Organic agriculture should be managed in a precautionary and responsible manner to protect the health and well-being of current and future generations and the environment.

## 4. Step-by-Step Conversion to Organic Farming

Conversion from a conventional farm to an organic system requires a planned transition period:

### Step 1: Good Information First
Farmers must acquire comprehensive knowledge on:
- How to restore and improve soil fertility without synthetic chemicals.
- How to keep crops healthy through cultural and biological mechanisms.
- How to maximize farm-level biodiversity and integrate livestock.
- Value addition, organic marketing, and certification norms.

### Step 2: Familiarize with Organic Practices (Small-Scale Trial)
- **Mulching**: Covering soil with dead organic material to control weeds, reduce evaporation, and shield soil biota.
- **Intercropping**: Pairing cereals with legumes (e.g., maize + beans) to optimize light, water, and nutrient capture without competition.
- **Composting**: Establishing on-farm aerobic compost units.
- **Green Manuring**: Growing fast-growing biomass crops for soil incorporation.
- **Leguminous Trees**: Planting trees such as *Gliricidia sepium*, *Calliandra*, and *Sesbania* in perennial stands (coffee, cocoa, banana) for nitrogen fixation, shade, and leaf mulch.
- **Soil Bunds & Terraces**: Establishing contours on slopes to halt erosion.

### Step 3: Full Farm Conversion
- Implementation of organic practices across the entire holding.
- Setting up **Buffer Zones & Hedgerows** along farm boundaries to prevent chemical drift from neighboring conventional farms.
- Strict prohibition of Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs) and synthetic off-farm inputs.
- Absolute avoidance of burning crop residues, as burning destroys soil microbiology, organic carbon, and earthworms.
