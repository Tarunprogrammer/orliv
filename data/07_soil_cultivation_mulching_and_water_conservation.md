# Organic Farming: Soil Cultivation, Mulching & Water Management

**Source**: Agronomy Department, CSA University of Agriculture & Technology, Kanpur (BS Global Publication House, 2023)

## 1. Soil Cultivation & Conservation Tillage

Soil cultivation encompasses mechanical actions to loosen, turn, and aerate soil (ploughing, tilling, digging, harrowing, hoeing).

### Objectives of Organic Soil Cultivation:
- Loosen compacted layers to facilitate deep root penetration.
- Enhance soil aeration, supplying atmospheric oxygen and nitrogen to roots and rhizosphere microbes.
- Stimulate beneficial soil organisms (earthworms, aerobic bacteria, mycorrhizae).
- Maximize rainwater infiltration capacity and reduce runoff erosion.
- Invert and incorporate crop residues, green manures, and organic composts into the biologically active top layer.
- Disrupt weed seedbeds and expose soil-dwelling pest pupae to predatory birds.

### Cultivation Precautions:
- **Avoid Over-Tillage**: Excessive pulverization accelerates organic humus oxidation and destroys crumb soil structure.
- **Avoid Mixing Soil Layers**: Do not invert deep subsoil into the biologically rich topsoil zone.
- Work the soil only when moisture levels are optimal (friable state) to prevent soil compaction and crusting.

## 2. Mulching in Organic Systems

Mulching is the practice of covering topsoil with plant biomass (dried leaves, dry grass, straw, cereal crop residues, sugarcane bagasse, sawdust, and twigs).

### Proven Functions & Benefits:
1. **Soil Moisture Conservation**: Substantially curbs transpirational and evaporative water losses from the root zone, reducing crop irrigation requirements by 30–50%.
2. **Erosion Shield**: Shields soil particles against kinetic raindrop impact and fierce rooftop/field winds.
3. **Soil Structure & Infiltration**: Eliminates surface soil crusting, maintaining open macropores for unhindered water absorption.
4. **Thermal Buffer**: Buffers soil against extreme diurnal temperature fluctuations (cools roots during scorching midday heat and retains warmth at night).
5. **Weed Suppression**: Thick organic mulch blocks sunlight from reaching weed seeds, arresting germination.
6. **Humus & Earthworm Food**: As earthworms and micro-fungi digest mulch, it steadily enriches the soil with humus and plant-available nutrients.

### Mulch Selection & Application Guidelines:
- **Fast-Decomposing Materials** (fresh grass, green leaves): Release nutrients rapidly but decompose within weeks; best for hungry vegetable crops.
- **Slow-Decomposing Materials** (straw, dry twigs, cereal husks): Provide prolonged soil protection and weed suppression over many months.
- **Vegetable Plots**: Apply mulch after young seedlings have developed and hardened slightly to avoid seedling scorch from fresh decomposition by-products.
- **Rainy Season Precaution**: Apply mulch before the onset of monsoon/heavy rains when soil is most susceptible to erosion.

## 3. Water Harvesting & Moisture Retention Strategies

1. **Planting Pits (Zai Pits)**:
   - Hand-dug circular pits measuring **20 cm across and 20 cm deep**.
   - Filled with compost and mulch, leaving the pit mouth partially open to capture and concentrate rainwater around the crop root zone.
2. **Contour Bunding & Catchment Strips**:
   - On gentle slopes (< 3%), contour bunds trap downhill runoff.
   - Alternating uncultivated catchment strips channel rainwater directly behind the contour bunds where crop rows are planted, ensuring successful harvests even in low-rainfall semi-arid zones.
3. **Shallow Inter-Row Hoeing (Dust Mulch)**:
   - Shallow scratch-digging of topsoil breaks capillary soil tubes, checking sub-surface moisture loss during dry spells.
