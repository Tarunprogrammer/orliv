# Organic Farming: Crop Planning, Intercropping & Seed Propagation

**Source**: Agronomy Department, CSA University of Agriculture & Technology, Kanpur (BS Global Publication House, 2023)

---

## 1. Crop Rotation Principles

Crop rotation—the practice of changing the type of crop grown in a bed or container each season—is the cornerstone of organic pest, weed, and soil fertility management.

### Key Rotation Rules & Benefits:
1. **Root Architecture Balance**:
   - Alternate deep-rooted crops (taproots that break hardpans and draw sub-surface minerals) with shallow-rooted crops (fibrous roots that bind topsoil and extract surface nutrients).
2. **Nutrient Demand & Fertility Cycles**:
   - Follow heavy nutrient feeders (e.g., maize, cabbage, tomatoes) with nitrogen-fixing legumes (beans, peas, cowpeas, groundnuts), followed by light-feeding root crops (carrots, radishes).
3. **Pest & Pathogen Disruption**:
   - *Never grow any crop after itself* or follow with a plant from the same botanical family (e.g., avoid planting tomato immediately after potato, eggplant, or capsicum).
   - Breaks reproductive life cycles of species-specific pests, nematodes, and soil-borne fungal pathogens (*Fusarium*, *Verticillium*).
4. **Cover Crops**:
   - Plant winter-killed cover crops (e.g., oat-pea mix) before early season spring vegetables for easy seedbed preparation without heavy tillage.

## 2. Intercropping Systems & Spatial Configurations

Intercropping involves growing two or more crops in close proximity to maximize ecological synergy, light interception, and spatial land productivity.

### Four Basic Spatial Arrangements:
1. **Row Intercropping**: Alternating rows of two different crops (e.g., alternating rows of cereal maize with climbing beans or cowpeas).
2. **Strip Intercropping**: Growing multi-row wide strips of different crops simultaneously to allow mechanized management while retaining ecological diversity.
3. **Relay Intercropping**: Sowing a second crop into the standing first crop before the first crop is harvested (e.g., broadcasting pulses into maturing paddy).
4. **Mixed Intercropping**: Growing two or more crops simultaneously with no distinct row arrangement (e.g., polyculture food gardens).

### Biological Push-Pull Strategy:
- **Example in Cereal Crops (Maize + Desmodium + Napier Grass)**:
  * **Intercropped Desmodium (Repellent Plant)**: Emits volatile chemicals that *"push"* away stem borers (*Chilo partellus*) and suppresses parasitic *Striga* weeds.
  * **Border Napier Grass (Trap Crop)**: Planted along field perimeter to *"pull"* pests by acting as an attractive decoy for egg-laying, while its sap immobilizes borer larvae.

## 3. Seed Selection, Traditional Varieties & Native Germplasm

- **Native & Traditional Varieties**: Possess natural evolutionary resilience against local insect pests, drought stress, and fungal pressures without requiring chemical inputs.
- **Genetic Diversity**: Sowing diverse open-pollinated heirloom cultivars ensures crop security and seed sovereignty.

## 4. Hot Water Seed Treatment Protocol (Thermal Disease Control)

Hot water seed treatment eliminates internal and surface-borne pathogens (e.g., *Alternaria*, *Colletotrichum*, *Phoma*, *Septoria*, *Pseudomonas*, *Xanthomonas*, black rot, black leg, and black scurf) without synthetic chemical fungicides.

### Standard Temperature and Immersion Durations:

| Crop Category / Seeds | Water Temp (°C) | Immersion Duration | Target Pathogens Controlled |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Potato Tubers & Banana Suckers** | **55°C** | **10 minutes** | Blackleg infection, powdery scab, black scurf, nematodes & banana weevils |
| **Spinach, Brussels Sprouts, Cabbage, Pepper, Tomato, Eggplant** | **50°C** | **30 minutes** | Black rot, bacterial canker, *Alternaria*, *Septoria* leaf spot, *Phoma* |
| **Broccoli, Cauliflower, Carrot, Collard, Kale, Kohlrabi, Turnip** | **50°C** | **20 minutes** | Black rot (*Xanthomonas*), bacterial leaf blight, downy mildew seeds |
| **Mustard, Cress, Radish** | **50°C** | **15 minutes** | Damping-off seed-borne spores, bacterial leaf spot |
| **Lettuce, Celery, Celeriac** | **47°C** | **30 minutes** | Bacterial leaf spot, Septoria celery blight, fungal mycelia |

> **Critical Handling Rule**: Always use an accurate laboratory thermometer and maintain exact water temperature with continuous stirring. Immediately plunge treated seeds into clean cold water after treatment to stop heating, then spread on a clean cloth in the shade to dry before sowing.
