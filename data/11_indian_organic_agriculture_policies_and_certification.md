# Organic Farming in India: Certification, Government Schemes & Statistics

**Source**: Agronomy Department, CSA University of Agriculture & Technology, Kanpur (BS Global Publication House, 2023)

---

## 1. National Organic Agriculture Statistics (APEDA / Ministry of Agriculture)

India is a global powerhouse in organic agriculture:
- **Global Rank in Organic Farmers**: **1st in the World** (over **1,599,010 (1.59 million) certified farmers**).
- **Global Rank in Organic Land**: **9th in the World** (over **4.33 million hectares** total area under organic certification).
  * *Cultivated Agricultural Area*: **2,657,889.33 hectares (2.65 million ha)**.
  * *Wild Harvest Collection Area*: **1,681,295.61 hectares (1.68 million ha)**.
- **Top Organic States in India**:
  1. **Madhya Pradesh**: Largest organic area in India (~1.63 million hectares).
  2. **Rajasthan**: ~481,862 hectares.
  3. **Maharashtra**: ~371,798 hectares.
  4. **Chhattisgarh**: ~286,684 hectares.
  5. **Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Karnataka, and Uttar Pradesh**.
- **100% Organic State Milestone**: In **2016, Sikkim** became the world's first 100% organic state by converting its entire cultivable land (> 75,000 hectares) under organic certification.

### Production and Export Value:
- **Total Production**: Around **3,496,800.34 MT (3.49 million MT)** of certified organic products.
- **Total Exports (2020–21)**: **888,179.68 MT** valued at **INR 707,849.52 Lakhs (~1,040.95 Million USD)**.
- **Key Export Destinations**: USA, European Union, Canada, Great Britain, Switzerland, Korea Republic, Israel, Australia, Ecuador, and Vietnam.
- **Export Commodity Share**:
  * Processed Foods (including Soya Meal): **57%**
  * Oilseeds: **9%**
  * Cereals & Millets: **7%**
  * Plantation Crops (Tea & Coffee): **6%**
  * Spices & Condiments: **5%**
  * Medicinal/Herbal & Aromatic Plants: **5%**
  * Dry Fruits & Sugar: **3% each**

## 2. NPOP Standards, APEDA Tracenet & Certification Process

The **National Programme for Organic Production (NPOP)** is implemented under the Ministry of Commerce & Industry (APEDA) and recognized by the European Commission and Switzerland as equivalent standards.

### Key Rules for Organic Certification:
- **Produce vs. Land**: The produce from the land is certified as organic, not the physical land itself.
- **Grower Groups**: Smallholder farmers (minimum of 25 to maximum of 500 farmers in the same geographical cluster) can form an Internal Control System (ICS) group for collective certification.
- **APEDA Tracenet**: An online e-service used to collect, record, track, and trace all certified organic produce from farm gate to international destination.
- **Certificate Validity**: An organic certificate issued by an accredited certification body is valid for **3 years** and must be renewed periodically.

### Critical Steps in Field Inspection:
1. Physical verification of fields, buffer zones, and storage facilities.
2. Complete audit of farm input records and production estimates.
3. Verification of strict non-use of Genetically Modified (GM) seeds and synthetic chemicals.
4. Laboratory residue testing of soil, irrigation water, and leaf tissues for heavy metals and banned pesticides.

## 3. Major Indian Government Schemes Promoting Organic Agriculture

### 1. Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY):
- Promotes cluster-based organic farming with **PGS (Participatory Guarantee System)** certification.
- **Financial Assistance**: **Rs. 50,000 per hectare for 3 years**, of which **62% (Rs. 31,000)** is provided directly as an incentive to farmers for purchasing organic inputs (bio-fertilizers, neem cake, vermicompost).
- Supports 40,000+ clusters covering ~7 lakh hectares.

### 2. Mission Organic Value Chain Development for North Eastern Region (MOVCDNER):
- Promotes third-party certified organic farming for niche crops in the 8 North-Eastern states with export focus.
- **Assistance**: **Rs. 25,000 per hectare for 3 years** for organic inputs, plus up to **Rs. 2 Crore** support for Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs) for post-harvest infrastructure and processing.

### 3. Capital Investment Subsidy Scheme (CISS) under Soil Health Management:
- **100% financial assistance up to Rs. 190 Lakh per unit** (3,000 Total Per Annum TPA capacity) to State Governments/agencies for establishing mechanized fruit/vegetable agro-waste compost units.
- **Up to 33% subsidy (up to Rs. 63 Lakh per unit)** for private entrepreneurs and individuals.

### 4. National Mission on Oilseeds and Oil Palm (NMOOP) & National Food Security Mission (NFSM):
- Provides **50% financial subsidy (up to Rs. 300 per hectare)** for the supply of bio-fertilizers (*Rhizobium*, Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria - PSB, Zinc Solubilizing Bacteria - ZSB, *Azotobacter*, Mycorrhiza, and vermicompost).

## 4. Key National & International Organic Organizations

- **International**:
  * **IFOAM** (International Federation of Organic Agriculture Movements, Germany, 1972)
  * **FiBL** (Research Institute of Organic Agriculture, Switzerland, 1973)
  * **WWOOF** (Worldwide Opportunities on Organic Farms, England, 1971)
  * **OFRF** (Organic Farming Research Foundation, California, 1990)
  * **IOIA / IOAS** (International Organic Inspectors / Accreditation Services)
- **National & Institutional**:
  * **APEDA** (Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority, New Delhi, 1985)
  * **NCOF / NIOF** (National Center / National Institute of Organic Farming, Ghaziabad, 2003/2004)
  * **OFAI** (Organic Farming Association of India, Goa, 2002)
  * **ICCOA** (International Competence Centre for Organic Agriculture, Bangalore, 2004)
  * **Prominent NGOs**: Navdanya (New Delhi, seed conservation), Haritika (MP), Manuvikasa (Karnataka), SEVA (Tamil Nadu), DHAN Foundation (Madurai).
